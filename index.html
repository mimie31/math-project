Application of Derivatives in Malaysian Palm Oil Yields

Analyzing Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) weight accumulation rates using calculus.

The Bioscience Problem

In Malaysia, maximizing the efficiency of Elaeis guineensis (Oil Palm) is vital for national and global food security. A fresh fruit bunch takes about 20 to 22 weeks to mature after anthesis (flowering). Understanding the rate of change of bunch weight accumulation helps agronomists optimize fertilizer schedules and accurately predict harvest windows when oil content peaks.

Data Reference Note: Growth curve parameters are adapted from physiological models matching standard Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB) agronomic yield data patterns (e.g., physiological studies featured in the Journal of Oil Palm Research (JOPR)).

Growth Function $f(x)$

Models total bunch weight (kg) over time $x$ (weeks):

$$f(x) = \frac{25}{1 + e^{-0.4(x - 12)}}$$

Where 25kg is the typical maximum asymptotic bunch mass, and 12 weeks is the midpoint of fastest growth.

Derivative Function $f'(x)$ (Rate of Growth)

Calculates the exact velocity of weight gain (kg/week) at week $x$:

$$f'(x) = \frac{10 \cdot e^{-0.4(x - 12)}}{\left(1 + e^{-0.4(x - 12)}\right)^2}$$

Derived via the Chain Rule and Quotient Rule applied to the logistic growth model.
